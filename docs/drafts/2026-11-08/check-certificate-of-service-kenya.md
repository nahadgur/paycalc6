# How to Check a Certificate of Service in Kenya

A certificate of service records your employment history. Check the employer, your name, employment dates, work description and usual workplace before filing it away. It is different from a reference, a final-pay statement and your P9 tax certificate.

If you are also checking final ordinary salary, use the [PAYE calculator](/). It does not establish a certificate entitlement or calculate every item in a termination settlement. The [employment situations guide](/guides/employment-situations) connects the separate exit checks.

## Who should receive a certificate?

Section 51 of the Employment Act requires an employer to issue a certificate on termination, except where employment lasted less than four consecutive weeks. The same section distinguishes that certificate from a testimonial or performance reference, which the employer is not generally bound to provide under that provision.

Ask for a certificate of service specifically. A request for a “recommendation letter” can be interpreted as a request for an assessment of your performance rather than the factual document you need.

## Check the statutory particulars

The section lists the employer's name and postal address, the employee's name, commencement date, nature and usual place of employment, cessation date and any other prescribed particulars. Compare these against your employment records rather than relying on memory.

Check a misspelt name against your onboarding records. Compare the start date with the appointment documents and the cessation date with the exit correspondence. A last day physically in the office can differ from the end of employment; ask HR to explain the date used where notice or leave creates a difference.

For someone who moved between roles, ask whether the description accurately records the work. Do not assume a new job title means a new start date. If the employing company changed, ask HR which employer and period the document covers instead of combining records without explanation.

## Keep it separate from the money documents

A certificate of service does not show whether all final salary, leave or notice amounts were paid. Use the [employee exit payroll checklist](/blog/employee-exit-payroll-checklist-kenya) for that calculation and payment trail.

Likewise, it does not replace the tax figures needed to [check a P9 against payslips](/blog/check-p9-against-payslips). Maintain a small exit folder with distinct documents for employment history, final-pay calculation, payment evidence and tax records. One document cannot reliably answer all four questions.

## Request a correction without altering the original

Send HR a short numbered list of discrepancies, identifying what the certificate says, the correction requested and the supporting document. For example: “The start date shown is 1 August; my appointment and first payslip record 1 July. Please check and issue a corrected certificate if appropriate.”

Retain the original and the response. Ask for a replacement issued by the employer rather than editing the PDF yourself. Keep the corrected version clearly identified so an old copy is not accidentally sent with a job application.

## If it has not been issued

Write to HR using a contact you can retain after work-email access ends. Include your employment period, employee reference if relevant, the request for a certificate of service and a safe delivery method. Keep evidence of the request and follow-up.

If the employer refuses or does not resolve the issue, take the documents to a labour officer, union representative or qualified employment adviser. Ask about the procedure and deadlines for your circumstances. Do not assume a generic online letter guarantees a particular outcome.
