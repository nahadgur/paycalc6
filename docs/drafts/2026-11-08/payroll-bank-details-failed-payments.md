# Payroll Bank Details: Safe Changes and Failed Payments

Change salary-payment details through your employer's approved private process, obtain confirmation of the payroll period affected and check the first payment. If salary does not arrive, establish whether the instruction was never submitted, rejected, returned or sent to the wrong destination before requesting another transfer.

Use the [PAYE calculator](/) to check the expected salary calculation. It cannot trace a bank transfer: a correct net-pay figure and a successful payment are separate checks.

## Submit a clear change request

Ask payroll which secure channel and evidence it accepts. Supply the account-holder name, bank and account information required by that process, plus the intended effective month. Do not send passwords, PINs or one-time codes. A colleague should not need access to your banking login to update the employer's payment instructions.

Section 17 of the Employment Act includes payment into a bank or building-society account designated by the employee. Ask payroll to retain your designation and verify any subsequent change independently. The steps here are recommended controls, not a prescribed universal bank form.

For employers, a second reviewer should compare the approved instruction with the payroll master record. Confirm unexpected changes using contact details already held on file rather than a new phone number supplied in the change message. Keep a record of who checked the request and when it became effective.

## Confirm which payment will use the new account

A request arriving after a payroll file is prepared may need separate handling. Ask whether the file has already been released and which account will receive that month's pay. Do not close the old account solely because you sent an email requesting a change.

A useful confirmation records the effective payroll month, masked account ending and whether any payment remains in progress. Keep the acknowledgement privately. The [new employee payroll checklist](/blog/new-employee-payroll-checklist-kenya) covers initial setup; this workflow is for a later change or failed payment.

## Trace a missing credit without duplicating salary

Compare the [payslip's net amount](/blog/how-to-read-your-kenyan-payslip) with actual credits. Send payroll the period, expected amount and payment date, asking for a transaction reference and confirmed status. Share only the relevant part of a bank record, through the approved channel.

If payroll says the bank rejected or returned the payment, ask for the rejection reason and the proposed correction. If it reports successful payment, ask the bank to trace that reference. A screenshot showing a payment instruction is not the same evidence as confirmation of its final status.

For example, if net salary is KES 70,000 and a KES 70,000 transfer is returned, a replacement KES 70,000 settles the original amount; it does not represent KES 140,000 earned. Keep the original, return and replacement references together. If both transfers arrive, tell payroll promptly and follow the [salary-overpayment reconciliation process](/blog/salary-overpayments-payroll-reconciliation).

## Close the issue with evidence

Record the actual credit date and amount. Confirm that future payments use the correct account and that no duplicate salary or recovery has been entered. Ask payroll separately about any tax-record adjustment rather than changing figures yourself merely because a transfer failed.

If salary remains unpaid, preserve the communications and seek advice from a labour officer, union or qualified adviser on the appropriate route. The [employment situations guide](/guides/employment-situations) helps distinguish payment problems from disputed deductions or pay entitlements.
